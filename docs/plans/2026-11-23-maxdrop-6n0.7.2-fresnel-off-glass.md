# Fresnel-off glass reflects its weight Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** Use subagents when appropriate to implement this plan. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** a VRayMtl with Fresnel off on refracting glass reflects its weight w at every angle and refracts the rest
(V-Ray's split, w + (1 - w) T), instead of Blender's glass Fresnel (full glass: the lone Principled; partial glass:
its refracting share's Fresnel under the mirror).

**Architecture:** em3.120's mirror (Mix Shader `Reflection weight` by w, second shader the white-metal copy
`Mirror reflection`) keeps its second shader; on a refracting Principled (`refracting()`, em3.135's rule) its first
shader becomes em3.135's `refraction_twin` (a Refraction BSDF, under the Principled twin with Transmission Weight 0
by a partial transmission) and the factor goes through `tir_weight` on a solid (1 under total internal reflection:
the white metal then reflects 1, as the glass does). `specular()` drops the lone path for Fresnel off on a constant
Transmission Weight >= 1. The filled Principled then feeds nothing (its inputs still hold the material's values; the
twins copy them). A Fresnel-off glass that `refracting()` refuses (metallic, alpha, emission, coat, sheen) takes the
mirror over the filled BSDF (still reflecting as glass under it): w + (1 - w)(F + (1 - F) T), closer to V-Ray than
the lone Fresnel for any w above F; decided, stated in docs. A constant w >= 1 with Fresnel off stays lone (from a
VRayMtl it cannot arrive: the metal rule makes a bright Fresnel-off reflection a metal).

**Tech Stack:** Blender 4.2 / 4.5 / 5.2 headless (Cycles, EEVEE), plain Python unittest.
